Get started in five steps

How to use KVoice on your Mac

From install to your first AI-finished text in about five minutes, most of it waiting for the speech model to download.

  1. 1

    Install KVoice

    The quickest way is Homebrew. In Terminal, run:

    brew install --cask kccarlos/tap/kvoice

    No Homebrew? Download the DMG from the latest GitHub release, open it, drag KVoice to Applications, and open it. It is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

    Needs a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 15 or later. Update later with brew upgrade --cask kvoice.

    Terminal

    ~ % brew install --cask kccarlos/tap/kvoice

  2. 2

    Follow the setup guide

    KVoice has no Dock icon; it lives in the menu bar. The first time it opens, a setup guide takes you through six steps:

    1. Welcome: a short introduction to how KVoice works.
    2. Speech model: downloads the recommended Whisper model (about 650 MB). The first load takes a few minutes while Core ML compiles it for the Neural Engine, once.
    3. Microphone: macOS asks for permission, and you can test the level.
    4. Accessibility: lets KVoice put text into other apps. The guide opens the right page of System Settings; turn KVoice on there and come back.
    5. Shortcut: record your own, or use the recommended Control-Shift-Space, then try it.
    6. Ready: you can start dictating.

    You can skip a step and come back later. KVoice never asks for a permission at launch, and without Accessibility it still transcribes and copies the text for you to paste.

  3. 3

    Hold the shortcut, speak, let go

    1. Click into a text field in any app.
    2. Hold the shortcut and speak. The recorder appears and shows what it hears, without taking focus from your app.
    3. Let go. KVoice transcribes on your Mac and the text appears at the cursor. It does not paste, so your clipboard stays as it was.

    Press Escape to cancel, and nothing is inserted. If there is nowhere to put the text (no text field, or a password field), KVoice copies it and tells you to press ⌘V.

    “AI off” on the recorder means plain dictation: what you said is what gets typed.

  4. 4

    Turn on AI Actions and pick a default

    AI Actions are off by default. Click the KVoice icon in the menu bar, switch on Use AI Actions, and pick a default action such as Clean Up or Email Draft. Every dictation then runs through it before it is typed; if the AI fails or times out, KVoice inserts your original transcript.

    Choose the engine first, in Settings›AI Actions:

    • Apple Intelligence (on-device), on macOS 26 or later: nothing to fill in, and nothing leaves your Mac.
    • An OpenAI-compatible endpoint: Ollama (local), OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Groq, Cerebras, Azure OpenAI or a custom URL, with your own key.

    While recording, ⌘1–⌘0 switch the action for that dictation (not in the App Store edition while another app is in front). With action triggers on, you can also start a sentence with a trigger word such as “draft email”, “translate” or “todo list”.

  5. 5

    Choose the speech model for what you say

    The recommended Whisper large-v3-turbo understands about a hundred languages and suits most people. In Speech Models you can compare, download and switch to others. Every model runs on your Mac, and only the default one is loaded into memory.

    • Mostly Chinese: try SenseVoice Small or Paraformer-large.
    • European languages, and speed: Parakeet TDT v3.
    • macOS 26 or later: Apple Speech is downloaded and managed by macOS, with nothing for KVoice to download.

    Every download is checked against a manifest built into the app; a file that does not match is refused. Add names and jargon to the Dictionary so they come out spelled your way.

Recording modes

Push-to-Talk, Toggle or Hybrid

Choose in Settings›Shortcuts. For long dictation, Toggle frees your hands.

Push-to-Talk

Hold to record, release to stop. The default.

The recorder says: Release to stop

Toggle

Press once to start, again to stop. Good for long dictation.

The recorder says: Press again to stop

Hybrid

A quick tap starts a hands-free recording that the next tap stops; a press-and-hold works as push-to-talk.

More triggers

The full edition can also use a modifier key on its own (such as Right Option) or the middle mouse button. Shortcuts, Siri and Spotlight can start and stop dictation too.

Two recorder styles

Choose in Settings›Recording›Recorder: Mini, a small floating panel low on the screen, or Notch, which grows out of the notch on Macs that have one. Neither takes focus.

AI Actions

Thirteen built-in AI actions, all editable

Edit any of them (Reset restores the original), or write your own. You can also write a short user profile, such as your role and how you like to write, which is sent with every request. The first ten can be picked with ⌘1–⌘0 while you record.

⌘1🧹Clean Up

Minimal cleanup: remove fillers and false starts; keep wording, tone, and structure.

⌘2✨Polish

Improve clarity and flow, fix grammar and recognition slips; keep the language mix.

⌘3💬Message

Rewrite as a clear, courteous message ready to send.

⌘4📝Notes

Restructure spoken thinking into short, grouped notes.

⌘5🧠Prompt

Turn a spoken request into a precise prompt for an AI assistant.

⌘6✍️Writing

Formal rewrite: polite, concise, fixes homophone and recognition slips, keeps details.

Trigger: “formal writing”
⌘7✉️Email Draft

Polite, concise email with greeting and sign-off; facts intact.

Trigger: “draft email”
⌘8📋Summarize

Concise bullets that keep every decision, number, and name.

Trigger: “summarize”
⌘9☑️TODO List

Turn speech into a checklist of actionable tasks; skip cancelled items.

Trigger: “todo list”
⌘0❓Q&A

Answer the dictated question directly; steps or code only when needed.

Trigger: “question”
⌨️Terminal

Speech to one safe shell command. Nothing is executed.

Trigger: “terminal”
🌐Translate

Lightly clean the speech and translate it into the target language.

Trigger: “translate”
🔁Translate 2

A second translation target for two-way conversations without switching back and forth.

Trigger: “translate two”

Trigger words work once action triggers are on (the default trigger words are English and can be edited). Any action can be set to include your clipboard text or selected text as context; both are off unless you turn them on. The full edition also has up to three Selection Actions shortcuts that run an action on text selected in any app and replace it.

Where AI runs

Your choice of AI engine

Save several configurations and switch from the menu bar. Verify & Save and Test Active Configuration send one short fixed test request, never a transcript.

Apple Intelligence on this Mac

macOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on. No account, no key, nothing leaves the Mac. A very long transcript may not fit the on-device model; KVoice then inserts it as is and tells you.

Any OpenAI-compatible service

Ollama (local), OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Groq, Cerebras, Azure OpenAI or a custom URL. Remote endpoints must use HTTPS; plain HTTP is accepted only for localhost.

Private Cloud Compute (App Store edition)

On macOS 27 or later, where Apple makes it available. You choose it explicitly; the text is processed on Apple's servers. Never used automatically, and never as a fallback for another engine.

Which edition should I choose?

Both editions come from the same code and both are free. Mac App Store apps must run in Apple's App Sandbox, which does not let one app read or change another app's text fields, so the App Store edition types your words as keystrokes and leaves out what needs to read other apps. Each edition keeps its own settings and history.

Permissions, in plain words

PermissionWhy KVoice asksWhen
MicrophoneTo hear you while you hold the shortcut. Audio is processed on your Mac.Your first dictation, or the microphone test in setup
AccessibilityTo put the text into the app you are using. The App Store edition uses only the keystroke part and cannot read other apps' text.During setup, before the first insertion

KVoice asks for these two permissions only; it does not need Input Monitoring (macOS may also ask about the local network if your AI server runs on another machine).

FAQ

KVoice for Mac: questions

Where is the app after I open it?

In the menu bar. KVoice has no Dock icon. Click its menu-bar icon for the state, AI Actions, the language, History and Settings.

The first dictation takes minutes.

That is Core ML compiling the model for your Mac, once per model (and after changing the processor in the Runtime card). Later loads are fast.

Does it work in every app?

It works in most apps that accept typed text, terminals included. When there is nowhere to put the text, KVoice copies it to the clipboard and tells you.

Is my clipboard used?

Not when insertion succeeds. The clipboard is written only when KVoice cannot put the text anywhere, or when you ask it to copy.

Can I use my own AI model?

Yes. Anything that speaks the OpenAI Chat Completions API works, including a local Ollama server, OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, OpenRouter, Groq, Cerebras and Azure OpenAI. Plain http:// is accepted only for localhost.

How do I switch the interface to Chinese?

It follows the system language by default. You can also pick Simplified Chinese in Settings › General › Interface language; KVoice offers to relaunch.

How do I uninstall?

With Homebrew: brew uninstall --cask kvoice, or brew uninstall --zap --cask kvoice to also remove its settings, models and history. Otherwise: quit KVoice, delete it from Applications, and delete the ~/Library/Application Support/kvoice/ folder, then run defaults delete io.github.kccarlos.kvoice.
